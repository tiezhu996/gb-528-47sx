## Design Context

### Users

The primary users are theatre rigging programmers and safety reviewers working together at a desk during offline rehearsal planning. They need to compare cue timing, equipment limits, interlock evidence, and human approvals without mistaking the application for machinery control.

### Use Cases

- Maintain a compact model of flown devices and their physical operating limits.
- Assemble cue actions, dependencies, and timing while spotting input mistakes early.
- Review versioned interlock rules and understand exactly which devices they affect.
- Run deterministic offline rehearsals, inspect collision windows, and record a human decision.

### Brand Personality

Disciplined backstage craft: direct, measured, alert, and evidence-led. The interface should feel like a well-marked stage manager cue sheet paired with an engineering inspection bench, never like an entertainment landing page or a live control console.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use a light technical-paper canvas, dark ink navigation, ruler-like timeline marks, amber review signals, red blocker labels, and a restrained blue-green confirmation color. Keep tables and timelines dense but legible. Avoid gradients, neon control-room styling, theatre photography, oversized headings, decorative cards, and anything that implies commands are being sent to equipment.

### Design Principles

1. Keep the offline, no-command safety boundary visible throughout the workspace.
2. Make cue order, time windows, versions, and approval ownership easy to scan.
3. Present every warning or blocker with text evidence, never color alone.
4. Use stable track dimensions so changing results cannot shift the page.
5. Preserve all essential actions and evidence on narrow screens through stacked layouts.
